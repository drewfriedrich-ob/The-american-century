December 24, 1968 · Apollo 8

Earthrise

On their fourth orbit of the Moon, the crew of Apollo 8 looked up and saw Earth rising over the lunar horizon. Bill Anders grabbed a camera. His photograph showed humanity its home as one small, fragile world.

“Good night, good luck, a Merry Christmas, and God bless all of you, all of you on the good Earth.”

Frank Borman · Apollo 8 commander

Epilogue

1968

Let’s end our journey where Apollo 8 found itself on Christmas Eve, 1968. Frank Borman, Jim Lovell and Bill Anders were the first human beings ever to leave Earth’s orbit. On their fourth pass around the Moon, Earth came up over the lunar horizon, blue and white against the black of space. Anders grabbed a camera loaded with color film, and the picture he took, now called Earthrise, became one of the most famous photographs ever made. That evening, the crew read the first ten verses of Genesis to a worldwide audience, and Borman closed the broadcast with the words on this slide. After a century of conflict, the most powerful image of the age was not a weapon. It was a picture of home, a reminder that every nation shares one small and fragile world.
