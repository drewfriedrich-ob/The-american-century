Act II · Between the Wars

1938

Watch the staircase climb. Each step was an act of aggression, and at each step the democracies looked away or gave way. The League of Nations protested when Japan took Manchuria and when Italy invaded Ethiopia, but did nothing effective. Hitler sent troops into the demilitarized Rhineland in 1936 and later admitted he would have retreated if France had resisted. In 1938 he absorbed Austria, then demanded the Sudetenland, the German-speaking border region of Czechoslovakia. At Munich, Britain’s Neville Chamberlain and France’s Édouard Daladier agreed, without Czechoslovakia at the table, and Chamberlain returned home promising “peace for our time.” Churchill called it “a total and unmitigated defeat.” Six months later Hitler took the rest of Czechoslovakia. In August 1939, Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union stunned the world with a non-aggression pact that secretly divided Poland between them. That was the last step. This policy of giving in to avoid war is called appeasement, and it remains one of history’s most studied leadership failures.
