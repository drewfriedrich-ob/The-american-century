Act I · The Great War

1917

For nearly three years the United States stayed neutral. Most Americans wanted no part of Europe’s war, and Wilson won the 1916 election on the slogan “He kept us out of war.” But pressure built. The sinking of the Lusitania in 1915 killed 128 Americans and outraged the public. American trade and loans increasingly tied the country to an Allied victory. In February 1917 Germany gambled: it resumed unrestricted submarine warfare, betting it could starve Britain before America could make a difference. Then British codebreakers intercepted the Zimmermann Telegram, in which Germany’s foreign minister proposed that Mexico join a war against the United States in exchange for Texas, New Mexico and Arizona. When it was published on March 1, the country was furious. On April 2, Wilson asked Congress for war, and on April 6, 1917, Congress declared it.
