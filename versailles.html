June 28, 1919 · Exactly five years after Sarajevo

A peace that planted the next war

The Treaty of Versailles

War guilt. Germany must accept blame for the war.

Reparations. Payments later set at 132 billion gold marks.

Land. Germany loses about 13% of its European territory and all its colonies.

Arms. Its army is capped at 100,000 men, with no air force and no tanks.

Wilson’s dream, a League of Nations, was born. But the U.S. Senate rejected the treaty, and America never joined.

Meanwhile, in a German army hospital

A 29-year-old corporal, temporarily blinded by a British gas attack, learned that Germany had surrendered. He blamed traitors at home, and he never forgave the peace.

His name was Adolf Hitler.

Act I · The Great War

1919

The peace treaty was signed in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles on June 28, 1919, exactly five years to the day after the assassination in Sarajevo. Germany was not invited to negotiate. It was forced to accept a war guilt clause, pay enormous reparations, give up territory and colonies, and shrink its military. Woodrow Wilson had arrived in Paris as a hero with his Fourteen Points and a vision of a League of Nations to keep the peace. He got the League, but the U.S. Senate refused to ratify the treaty, partly over fears that the League would drag America into future wars, and the United States never joined. Meanwhile, many Germans came to believe the myth that their army had not been beaten in the field but “stabbed in the back” by politicians at home.
