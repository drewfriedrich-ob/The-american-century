Act III · World War II

1945

After D-Day, the Allies broke out of Normandy in late July and liberated Paris on August 25, 1944. Many believed the war would be over by Christmas. Instead, on December 16, Hitler launched a surprise offensive through the snowy Ardennes forest, creating a “bulge” in the American lines. At the crossroads town of Bastogne, the 101st Airborne was surrounded. When the Germans demanded surrender, Brigadier General Anthony McAuliffe replied, “Nuts!” His men held until General George Patton’s Third Army broke through. The Battle of the Bulge was the largest and bloodiest battle fought by the U.S. Army in the war. In February 1945, Roosevelt, Churchill and Stalin met at Yalta to plan the peace. Roosevelt died on April 12; Harry Truman, who had not even been told about the atomic bomb project, became president. American and Soviet soldiers met on the Elbe River on April 25. Hitler killed himself on April 30, and Germany surrendered unconditionally. May 8, 1945, became V-E Day, Victory in Europe. But the war with Japan was not over.
