The 1920s and 1930s

The rise of the dictators

Depression, resentment and fear opened the door. Strongmen walked through it.

Italy · 1922

Benito Mussolini

Founder of fascism. Seized power after his March on Rome and dreamed of a new Roman empire.

Soviet Union · 1920s

Joseph Stalin

Ruled by terror. Forced farm collectivization, famine and purges killed millions of his own people.

Germany · 1933

Adolf Hitler

Appointed chancellor, he destroyed German democracy within months and vowed to tear up Versailles.

Japan · 1930s

The militarists

Army leaders seized control of policy and set out to build an empire across Asia and the Pacific.

Stalin would be Hitler’s partner in 1939, and his deadliest enemy by 1941.

Act II · Between the Wars

1933

While America struggled through the Depression, much of the world turned to dictators. Mussolini took power in Italy in 1922 and gave the world the word fascism. Stalin consolidated his grip on the Soviet Union by the late 1920s; his forced collectivization of farms caused a famine that killed millions, especially in Ukraine, and his purges in the late 1930s killed hundreds of thousands more. Hitler became German chancellor on January 30, 1933, and within months had banned other parties, crushed the free press and begun persecuting Jews. In Japan, military leaders increasingly dictated policy, and the army invaded Manchuria in 1931. What these regimes shared was a belief that might makes right, and a contempt for the democracies, which they believed were too weak and divided to stop them. For most of the 1930s, they were right.
