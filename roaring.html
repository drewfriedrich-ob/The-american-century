Act II · Between the Wars

1925

After the war, Americans wanted, in President Warren Harding’s phrase, a “return to normalcy.” The 1920s brought women the vote, mass-produced cars, radio, movies, jazz and a booming stock market. Prohibition, which banned alcohol from 1920 to 1933, unintentionally made gangsters like Al Capone rich. The Harlem Renaissance produced some of the most important writers, musicians and artists in American history. Charles Lindbergh’s solo flight to Paris in 1927 made him the most famous man in the world. But beneath the glitter were warning signs: farmers were struggling, wealth was concentrated, and millions of people were buying stocks with borrowed money. Then came October 1929.
