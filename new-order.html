Act III · World War II

1947

After 1918, the victors punished Germany and walked away. After 1945, America chose a different path, and it is one of the great success stories in modern history. The United Nations was founded in October 1945, with the United States as a leading member. At Nuremberg, Nazi leaders were tried before an international tribunal, establishing that individuals are responsible for crimes against humanity. At home, the GI Bill sent millions of veterans to college and helped them buy homes, building the modern middle class. The Marshall Plan poured about 13 billion dollars into rebuilding Europe, including West Germany, and within a few years the continent was booming. In 1948 Stalin cut off land access to West Berlin, hoping to force the Allies out. Instead, American and British planes supplied the city by air for eleven months, at the peak landing about every minute. An American pilot named Gail Halvorsen began dropping candy on little handkerchief parachutes to the children watching at the fence; others joined in, and they dropped about 23 tons. That is what generosity looks like at national scale. But by 1949, the wartime alliance had become a standoff.
