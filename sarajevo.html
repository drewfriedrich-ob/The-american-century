June 28, 1914 · Sarajevo, Bosnia

A wrong turn that changed the world

10:10 a.m.

A bomb thrown at Archduke Franz Ferdinand’s open car bounces off and explodes under the car behind. The motorcade races on.

10:45 a.m.

After a City Hall reception, the archduke insists on visiting the wounded in hospital. No one tells his driver the route has changed.

Moments later

The driver turns onto the wrong street, stops, and starts to reverse, a few feet from a 19-year-old named Gavrilo Princip.

Two shots

The heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne and his wife, Sophie, are dead within the hour.

Act I · The Great War

1914

Sunday, June 28, 1914. Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, and his wife Sophie are touring Sarajevo in an open car. Several young assassins, Bosnian Serb nationalists connected to a secret Serbian society called the Black Hand, are waiting along the route. The first attack fails: a bomb bounces off the archduke’s car. After a tense reception at City Hall, Franz Ferdinand decides to visit the officers wounded by the bomb. But nobody tells his driver. The car turns onto Franz Joseph Street, the original route, someone shouts that it is wrong, and the driver stops to reverse. Standing nearby is Gavrilo Princip, just 19. He steps forward and fires twice. Times are approximate. Within the hour, both the archduke and Sophie are dead. Princip was too young for the death penalty under Austrian law; he died of tuberculosis in prison in 1918, months before the war he triggered ended.
