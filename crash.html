1929

On Black Tuesday, October 29, 1929, panic selling swept Wall Street. Watch the bars: the Dow Jones average peaked at 381 in September 1929, fell to 199 by mid-November, bounced back to 294 in April 1930 as people hoped the worst was over, and then slid for two more years to 41 in July 1932. That is a loss of about 89 percent. It would not regain its 1929 peak until 1954. The crash did not cause the Great Depression alone, but it helped trigger a cascade: bank runs, collapsing prices, mass unemployment that reached about a quarter of the workforce, and on the Great Plains, the drought and dust storms of the Dust Bowl. Franklin Roosevelt took office in March 1933 and launched the New Deal. The Depression was global, and in Germany and Japan desperation made people willing to listen to extremists.
