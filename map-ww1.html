1914

Watch the map fill in year by year. In August 1914 the Allied Powers were Britain, France, Russia, Belgium and Serbia, joined that month by Japan, which wanted Germany’s colonies in China and the Pacific. Against them stood the Central Powers: Germany and Austria-Hungary, joined in the autumn by the Ottoman Empire. Italy had been allied with Germany, but in 1915 it switched to the Allies in exchange for promises of territory. Bulgaria joined the Central Powers the same year. Romania and Portugal followed in 1916, Greece and the United States in 1917. Note Germany’s problem: it was squeezed between France in the west and Russia in the east, the two-front war its generals had feared for decades. This map uses today’s borders for orientation, which is a simplification: in 1914, for example, Poland did not exist as a country.
