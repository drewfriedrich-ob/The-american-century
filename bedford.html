June 6, 1944 · Bedford, Virginia

One small town, one terrible morning

Bedford, a Virginia town of about 3,200 people, lost 19 of its sons on D-Day. Most served together in Company A, 116th Infantry, in the first wave at Omaha Beach.

No American community is believed to have lost more, for its size, that day. It is why the National D-Day Memorial stands in Bedford.

Act III · World War II

1944

History becomes real when it has names. Company A of the 116th Infantry Regiment was a Virginia National Guard unit, and many of its men came from the small town of Bedford in the foothills of the Blue Ridge. On the morning of June 6, 1944, they were in the first wave at Omaha Beach, in a sector called Dog Green, where German fire was devastating. Nineteen Bedford men were killed that morning, and others died later in the Normandy campaign. Weeks later, the telegrams began arriving at the town’s Western Union office, one after another. Because Bedford is believed to have suffered the highest per capita D-Day loss of any community in America, Congress chose it as the site of the National D-Day Memorial, which opened in 2001. It is about a four-hour drive from Hampton Roads, and it is worth the trip.
