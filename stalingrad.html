Aug 1942 – Feb 1943 · Stalingrad

The largest tank battle ever fought. Afterward, Germany never again mounts a major offensive in the East.

Act III · World War II

1943

Americans often remember World War II through D-Day and the Pacific, but most of the war’s fighting and dying in Europe happened on the Eastern Front. On June 22, 1941, Hitler broke his pact with Stalin and invaded the Soviet Union with more than three million troops. German forces reached the outskirts of Moscow and besieged Leningrad for nearly 900 days, during which hundreds of thousands of civilians starved. The turning point came at Stalingrad on the Volga River, where from August 1942 to February 1943 the two armies fought building by building. A Soviet counteroffensive encircled the German Sixth Army, which surrendered on February 2, 1943. At Kursk that summer, the Soviets defeated Germany’s last great offensive in the East. The cost to the Soviet people was staggering, an estimated 24 to 27 million dead. Stalin was a brutal dictator, and an uneasy ally. But without the Soviet war effort, the Allies could not have defeated Germany when they did.
