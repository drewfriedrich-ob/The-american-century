Act III · World War II

1941

Unlike 1914, the motives in this war were starkly different on each side. Hitler’s goals were laid out in his own writings: overturn the Treaty of Versailles, conquer “living space,” Lebensraum, in Poland and the Soviet Union, and build a racial empire, which meant enslaving or murdering millions of people he considered inferior. Mussolini wanted a Mediterranean empire. Japan, with few natural resources of its own, sought to control the oil, rubber and minerals of Southeast Asia and to dominate China, which it had invaded in 1937. On the Allied side, Britain and France went to war because they had guaranteed Poland’s independence. The Soviet Union and China fought for survival. And the United States, after Pearl Harbor, fought for what Roosevelt in January 1941 had called the Four Freedoms: freedom of speech, freedom of worship, freedom from want and freedom from fear.
