Act I · The Great War

1918

When America declared war in April 1917, its army was smaller than Portugal’s by some counts. The country introduced a draft, and by the war’s end about four million Americans were in uniform, with more than two million in France under General John J. Pershing, commander of the American Expeditionary Forces. Their arrival came just as Germany launched its last great offensives in the spring of 1918. Three stories stand out. The Harlem Hellfighters, the 369th Infantry, were barred from fighting alongside white American units and were assigned to the French army instead; they spent about 191 days under fire. Sergeant Henry Johnson of the 369th received the Medal of Honor, posthumously, in 2015. And Alvin York, a young man from the hills of Tennessee whose faith made him wrestle with whether a Christian could kill, became the most famous American soldier of the war after his actions in the Argonne Forest. He later said he believed God had protected him that day, and he used his fame to build a school for children in his home county.
