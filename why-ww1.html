Act I · Motives

Why each nation went to war

Allied Powers

Serbia

Defend its independence against an Austro-Hungarian invasion.

Russia

Protect fellow Slavs in Serbia and its standing as a great power.

France

Stand by its ally Russia, and win back Alsace-Lorraine, lost to Germany in 1871.

Britain

Defend neutral Belgium, which it had promised to protect, and stop any one power from dominating Europe.

Central Powers

Austria-Hungary

Punish Serbia and crush the Slavic nationalism threatening to break its empire apart.

Germany

Back its one dependable ally, and strike before Russia and France grew stronger.

Ottoman Empire

Join Germany against its old enemy Russia, and recover lost prestige.

Bulgaria

Win back land it had lost to Serbia in the Balkan Wars of 1912–1913.

1914

Act I · The Great War

1914

It helps to see the war from each capital. Austria-Hungary was a patchwork empire of a dozen nationalities, and its leaders believed that if they did not crush Serbian nationalism, the empire would come apart. Germany backed its only reliable ally and believed time was against it, since Russia was modernizing fast. Russia saw itself as the protector of Slavic peoples. France was bound to Russia by treaty and had never accepted the loss of Alsace-Lorraine. Britain hesitated until Germany invaded Belgium, whose neutrality Britain had guaranteed since 1839. The Ottomans signed a secret alliance with Germany. Later entrants such as Italy, Bulgaria and Romania were largely bargaining for territory. Ask yourself: which of these motives would have been enough, on its own, to start a world war? Probably none. That is the tragedy of 1914.
