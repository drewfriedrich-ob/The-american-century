The American Century · 1898 to 1969

From the world stage
to the Moon

In seventy-one years, a young republic rose on industry, courage and faith, and paid dearly for its place in the world. At its best, it used that strength to rebuild former enemies and to reach for the stars.

“When I look at your heavens, the work of your fingers, the moon and the stars, which you have set in place, what is man that you are mindful of him, and the son of man that you care for him?”

Questions and conversation

So, back to the question we started with: how did a young republic become the most powerful nation on Earth, and what did it cost? In 1898, America was a rising industrial power that had barely stepped onto the world stage. Seventy-one years later, Americans were walking on the Moon. It got there through extraordinary industry, through the courage and sacrifice of ordinary people, many of them sustained by deep faith, and, at its best, through generosity: rebuilding former enemies rather than crushing them. It paid for that rise in lives and in families who carried the loss for generations. The psalmist looked at the night sky and asked what humanity is, that God should care for us. The astronauts of Apollo 8 looked back at Earth and asked something similar. It is a good question to carry out of this room. Thank you. I would love to hear your questions and reflections.
