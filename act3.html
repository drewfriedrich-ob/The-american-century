Act III · World War II

1939

Act Three: World War II. At 4:45 in the morning on September 1, 1939, the old German battleship Schleswig-Holstein, anchored at Danzig on a so-called courtesy visit, opened fire on the Polish garrison at Westerplatte. At the same time, German armies poured across the border. Two days later Britain and France declared war on Germany. This war would be fought on every inhabited continent and every ocean. It would kill more people than any conflict in history, the majority of them civilians. And it would end with a weapon that changed what war could mean. For the United States, it would be the defining experience of a generation.
