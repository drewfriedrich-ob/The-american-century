1914

Act I · The Great War

1914

Watch the fuse burn across the slide. This is the July Crisis. Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia for the assassination and, after Germany promised its full support, often called the “blank check,” issued an ultimatum designed to be refused. On July 28 it declared war. Russia saw itself as protector of the Slavic Serbs and mobilized. Germany’s war plan, the Schlieffen Plan, assumed a two-front war and called for knocking out France quickly by marching through neutral Belgium. Britain had guaranteed Belgium’s neutrality in an 1839 treaty, which a German official famously dismissed as “a scrap of paper.” On August 4, Britain declared war. It took 37 days to go from two pistol shots to a world war. Many soldiers on every side were told they would be home by Christmas.
