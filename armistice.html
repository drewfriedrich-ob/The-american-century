Act I · The Great War

1918

Germany signed the armistice in a railway carriage in the Compiègne Forest at about 5 a.m. on November 11, 1918, to take effect at 11 a.m. That is why we mark Veterans Day, originally Armistice Day, on November 11. The cost was almost beyond comprehension: roughly 20 million dead, soldiers and civilians, though estimates vary. More than 116,000 Americans died, many of them from disease, especially the influenza pandemic of 1918, which swept through crowded army camps and troopships and killed tens of millions worldwide, more than the war itself. Four great empires vanished: Germany’s Kaiser abdicated, Austria-Hungary broke apart, the Russian Tsar had been overthrown and later murdered, and the Ottoman Empire would soon be dismantled. And one detail captures the tragedy: Henry Gunther, an American private, charged a German position at 10:59 a.m. and was killed. He is often remembered as the last soldier of any army killed in the war.
