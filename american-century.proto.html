How did a young republic become the most powerful nation on Earth, and what did it cost?

Seventy-one years in four acts

Prologue · 1898

The year America stepped onto the world stage

In one summer, an inward-looking nation became a power with territory across two oceans.

Feb–Aug 1898

“A splendid little war”

After the USS Maine exploded in Havana harbor, the U.S. fought Spain and won in about four months, taking Puerto Rico, Guam and the Philippines.

July 1898

A Pacific foothold

Congress annexed Hawaii. Pearl Harbor would become the Navy’s great Pacific base, and, 43 years later, a target.

1904–1914

The big stick

Theodore Roosevelt pushed the Panama Canal into being. It opened on August 15, 1914, eleven days after Britain went to war.

The Great War

1914–1918 · “The war to end all wars”

“The lamps are going out all over Europe; we shall not see them lit again in our lifetime.”

Sir Edward Grey, British Foreign Secretary · August 3, 1914

Act I · The deep causes

Europe in 1914 was a powder keg

Historians sum up the long-burning causes in four letters.

M

Militarism

The great powers raced to build armies and navies, and every general had a war plan ready.

A

Alliances

Two armed camps. A fight with one could mean a fight with all.

I

Imperialism

The scramble for colonies in Africa and Asia bred suspicion and near-wars.

N

Nationalism

France wanted Alsace-Lorraine back. The Balkans became the fuse.

All it needed was a spark.

The July Crisis

37 days: from one gunshot to a world war
