Act III · World War II

1945

The Manhattan Project, a secret effort employing about 130,000 people, produced the first atomic bomb, tested in the New Mexico desert on July 16, 1945. Japan had rejected the Allied demand for unconditional surrender. After the bloody battles of Iwo Jima and Okinawa, American planners prepared for an invasion of Japan’s home islands that they feared would be catastrophic; nearly half a million Purple Heart medals were manufactured in anticipation, and the military was still awarding medals from that stock decades later. On August 6, a B-29 called the Enola Gay dropped a bomb on Hiroshima at 8:15 in the morning; many clocks and watches in the city stopped at that moment. Three days later, a second bomb destroyed much of Nagasaki. The Soviet Union declared war on Japan on August 8. On August 15, Emperor Hirohito announced Japan’s surrender by radio, and on September 2, 1945, Japan formally surrendered aboard the battleship Missouri in Tokyo Bay. Historians still debate whether the bombs were necessary. Reasonable, thoughtful people disagree, and it is worth sitting with both sides of that question.
