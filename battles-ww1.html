Act I · The Great War

1916

Four battles capture the war’s character. At the Marne in September 1914, the French and British stopped the German advance within about 30 miles of Paris; the French army famously commandeered roughly 600 Paris taxicabs to rush reinforcements to the front. At Gallipoli in 1915, Winston Churchill’s plan to force open the Dardanelles and knock the Ottoman Empire out of the war ended in costly failure; it forged national identity in Australia and New Zealand, and in Turkey it made the reputation of Mustafa Kemal, later Atatürk. Verdun in 1916 lasted about ten months and became a symbol of French endurance. And on the first day of the Somme, July 1, 1916, the British Army suffered 57,470 casualties, the bloodiest day in its history. By the end of 1916, both sides had lost staggering numbers of men for a few miles of mud.
