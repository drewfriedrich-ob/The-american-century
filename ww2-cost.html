Act III · World War II

1945

Let this chart sink in. Every dot represents one hundred thousand human beings. The Soviet Union lost something on the order of 27 million people, China perhaps 15 to 20 million, many from famine and disease as well as fighting. Poland lost about one in six of its people, including some three million Polish Jews. Germany lost roughly seven million, Japan about three million. The United States, protected by two oceans, lost about 420,000, which is just four dots on this chart, though each of those was a family that never recovered. Exact numbers are uncertain and historians’ estimates differ, especially for China and the Soviet Union, so treat these as rounded midpoints. The total is usually given as 70 to 85 million, roughly three percent of the world’s population at the time. Most of the dead were civilians.
