Act IV · The Cold War

1949

The Cold War was a contest between two systems. The United States stood for democracy and free markets; the Soviet Union for one-party communist rule and a state-run economy, and Stalin was determined never again to be invaded from the west. In 1947, President Truman pledged to support free peoples resisting subjugation, starting with Greece and Turkey. Diplomat George Kennan’s strategy of “containment,” stopping communism from spreading rather than trying to roll it back by force, became American policy for four decades. 1949 was a shock year: the Soviets tested their first atomic bomb, and Mao Zedong’s communists won China’s civil war. The arms race that followed produced tens of thousands of nuclear weapons, peaking around 70,000 worldwide in the mid-1980s. Because a direct war could end civilization, the superpowers competed everywhere else: in proxy wars, in espionage, in propaganda, and in space. The first proxy war came fast, in Korea.
