Act III · World War II

1940

Germany’s new way of war, called blitzkrieg or lightning war, combined tanks, motorized infantry and dive bombers to punch through enemy lines and keep moving. Poland fell in about five weeks, while the Soviet Union, under its secret pact with Hitler, invaded from the east. After a quiet winter, Germany took Denmark and Norway in April 1940, then on May 10 attacked the Low Countries and France. The same day, Winston Churchill became Britain’s prime minister. German tanks came through the Ardennes forest, which the French believed was impassable, and raced to the English Channel. At Dunkirk, a fleet of navy ships and hundreds of small civilian boats evacuated about 338,000 British and French soldiers. France surrendered on June 22. Britain stood alone, and in the summer and fall of 1940 the Royal Air Force defeated the German Luftwaffe in the Battle of Britain. Hitler’s planned invasion never came. Instead, he turned east.
