Jewish men, women and children were murdered by Nazi Germany and its collaborators, about two of every three Jews in Europe.

Millions of others were also killed: Roma, people with disabilities, Polish and Soviet civilians, prisoners of war, and others the regime deemed unworthy of life.

Act III · World War II

1945

This slide is deliberately still. The Holocaust was the systematic, state-sponsored murder of six million Jews by Nazi Germany and its collaborators, along with millions of others. It began with laws that stripped Jews of their rights, moved to ghettos and mass shootings, and ended in industrialized killing centers like Auschwitz-Birkenau, where about a million Jews were murdered. When American troops liberated camps in April 1945, General Eisenhower visited the Ohrdruf camp and insisted that soldiers, journalists and members of Congress see it and that everything be recorded, because he foresaw that some would one day claim it never happened. In the darkness there was also light. Israel’s Holocaust memorial, Yad Vashem, has recognized more than 28,000 Righteous Among the Nations, people who risked everything to save Jews. Corrie ten Boom, a Dutch Christian watchmaker, hid Jews in a secret room in her home; her family was arrested and her sister Betsie died in the Ravensbrück camp. Corrie survived and spent the rest of her life speaking about forgiveness. She often repeated her sister’s words: there is no pit so deep that God’s love is not deeper still.
