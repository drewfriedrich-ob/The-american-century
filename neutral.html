Act III · World War II

1941

After the losses of the First World War, many Americans concluded that entering it had been a mistake, and Congress passed Neutrality Acts in the mid-1930s to keep the country out of future wars. A powerful “America First” movement opposed any involvement. But as Hitler conquered Europe, Roosevelt edged the nation toward Britain. Cash and carry let the Allies buy weapons. In 1940 he traded 50 old destroyers for leases on British bases, and Congress approved the first peacetime draft in American history. In a December 1940 fireside chat he declared that America must be “the great arsenal of democracy.” Lend-Lease, in March 1941, sent enormous quantities of war material to Britain, and later to the Soviet Union and China. Meanwhile, to pressure Japan over its war in China and its move into French Indochina, the United States froze Japanese assets and cut off oil exports. Japan, which depended on American oil, faced a choice: back down or seize the oil fields of Southeast Asia. It chose war.
