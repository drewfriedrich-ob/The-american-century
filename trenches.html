This cross-section shows why the Western Front became a slaughterhouse. Defenders sat in deep trenches behind sandbags and coils of barbed wire, with machine guns covering the open ground in front. Attackers had to climb out and cross no man’s land, cratered by shellfire, often under the light of flares like the one you saw rise over the battlefield. New technology favored the defender for most of the war. Generals tried to break the deadlock with ever-larger artillery barrages, with poison gas, first used on a large scale by Germany near Ypres in April 1915, and with tanks, which the British introduced at the Somme in September 1916. Life in the trenches meant mud, rats, lice, trench foot, and the constant threat of shelling. Many soldiers rotated between the front line, support trenches and rest areas behind the lines.
