Act Two. Marshal Ferdinand Foch, the French general who commanded the Allied armies in 1918, looked at the Treaty of Versailles and reportedly predicted that it was not a peace but an armistice for twenty years. Watch the fuse on this slide burn through those twenty years, one mark per year. He was almost exactly right: the next world war began in September 1939. What happened in between? A decade of prosperity and fun in America, then the worst economic collapse in modern history, and, across the world, the rise of men who promised that strength and obedience would fix everything.
