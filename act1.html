1914

Act One. On the evening of August 3, 1914, British Foreign Secretary Sir Edward Grey stood at his office window watching the lamplighters at work in St. James’s Park, and, as he later recalled, said that the lamps were going out all over Europe. Watch the row of lights on this slide go dark one by one. That is what happened to a continent in a matter of days. Within four years, empires that had lasted centuries would be gone, roughly twenty million people would be dead, and the United States would emerge as a world power. So how does a continent at peace, connected by trade and royal family ties, tear itself apart? It starts with a powder keg.
