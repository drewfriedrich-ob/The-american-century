Act III · World War II

1941

Early on Sunday morning, December 7, 1941, two Army privates at the Opana radar station on Oahu saw the biggest blip they had ever seen, more than 130 miles to the north. They reported it, and a lieutenant, thinking it was a flight of American B-17 bombers expected from California, told them, “Don’t worry about it.” At 7:48 a.m. the first wave of Japanese planes from six aircraft carriers struck the Pacific Fleet. In less than two hours, the attack killed 2,403 Americans, including 68 civilians, and wounded more than a thousand. The battleship Arizona exploded and sank with 1,177 men; most remain entombed in her hull today. The next day Roosevelt called December 7 “a date which will live in infamy,” and Congress declared war on Japan with one dissenting vote. On December 11, Germany and Italy declared war on the United States. America was now in a two-ocean war. And here is the detail that changed everything: the Pacific Fleet’s aircraft carriers were not in port. Six months later, they would have their revenge.
