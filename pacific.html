Act III · World War II

1942

By mid-1942, Japan controlled an empire stretching from the borders of India to the islands of the central Pacific, the dashed outline on this map. Then came Midway. Navy codebreakers in Hawaii had partly broken Japan’s naval code and tricked the Japanese into confirming that their target, code-named “AF,” was Midway, by having the island send a fake message that its water plant had failed. Admiral Chester Nimitz set an ambush. On the morning of June 4, 1942, American dive bombers caught three Japanese carriers with planes and fuel on their decks; within minutes all three were burning. A fourth was sunk that afternoon. Japan never recovered the initiative. From there, Americans and their allies fought island by island: the brutal six-month campaign for Guadalcanal, Tarawa, Saipan, the Battle of Leyte Gulf, the largest naval battle in history, then Iwo Jima and Okinawa, ever closer to Japan.
