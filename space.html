Act IV · The Cold War

1969

The space race was the Cold War’s most peaceful contest. On October 4, 1957, the Soviet Union launched Sputnik, a satellite about the size of a beach ball, and Americans worried they had fallen behind. Congress created NASA in 1958. In 1961 Yuri Gagarin became the first person in space, and President Kennedy challenged the nation to land a man on the Moon before the decade was out. In February 1962, John Glenn became the first American to orbit Earth. Before he flew, he asked that Katherine Johnson, a mathematician at NASA Langley in Hampton, check the computer’s calculations by hand; her story is told in the book and film Hidden Figures. On Christmas Eve 1968, the crew of Apollo 8, the first humans to circle the Moon, took turns reading the opening verses of Genesis to a worldwide audience. Seven months later, on July 20, 1969, Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed in the Sea of Tranquility, while Michael Collins orbited overhead. Hundreds of millions of people watched on television. America had won the race, and for a moment, the whole world shared the achievement.
