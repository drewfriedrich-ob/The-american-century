Act III · World War II

1944

Operation Overlord was the largest seaborne invasion in history. After the weather forced a one-day delay, General Dwight Eisenhower gave the order: “OK, let’s go.” Overnight, paratroopers of the American 82nd and 101st Airborne and the British 6th Airborne dropped behind the beaches. At dawn on June 6, 1944, about 156,000 troops came ashore on five beaches: Americans at Utah and Omaha, British at Gold and Sword, Canadians at Juno. Omaha was the bloodiest; for hours the landing there hung in the balance. Research by the National D-Day Memorial Foundation counts 4,414 Allied dead on D-Day itself, 2,501 of them Americans. And here is a leadership lesson worth remembering: before the invasion, Eisenhower wrote a short note to be released if it failed. It ended, “If any blame or fault attaches to the attempt it is mine alone.” He put it in his wallet, and he never had to use it.
