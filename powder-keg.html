Act I · The deep causes

Europe in 1914 was a powder keg

Historians sum up the long-burning causes in four letters.

M

Militarism

The great powers raced to build armies and navies. Britain and Germany fought a battleship arms race, and every general had a war plan ready.

A

Alliances

Two armed camps: France, Russia and Britain on one side; Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy on the other. A fight with one could mean a fight with all.

I

Imperialism

The scramble for colonies in Africa and Asia bred suspicion and near-wars, including two crises over Morocco in 1905 and 1911.

N

Nationalism

France wanted Alsace-Lorraine back. Slavs in the Balkans wanted freedom from Austria-Hungary. The Balkans became the fuse.

All it needed was a spark.

1914

Act I · The Great War

1914

Students often learn the causes of the First World War through the acronym MAIN: militarism, alliances, imperialism and nationalism. Militarism: Germany and Britain were locked in a naval race to build dreadnought battleships, and each army had detailed mobilization timetables. Alliances: the Triple Entente of France, Russia and Britain faced the Triple Alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy. Imperialism: competition for colonies created constant friction. Nationalism: French resentment over losing Alsace-Lorraine in 1871, and Serbian and Slavic nationalism pulling at the multi-ethnic Austro-Hungarian Empire. None of these alone started the war. Together, they meant that a small crisis could become a catastrophe. The crisis came on a summer morning in Sarajevo.
