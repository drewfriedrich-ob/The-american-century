Act IV · The Cold War

1949

Act Four: the Cold War. In March 1946, at a small college in Fulton, Missouri, with President Truman in the audience, Winston Churchill warned that an “iron curtain” had descended across Europe. Watch the red line draw itself across the map. East of it, the Soviet Union installed communist governments in Poland, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria and East Germany. West of it, the democracies of Western Europe joined the United States in NATO in 1949, pledging that an attack on one would be an attack on all. The Soviets answered with the Warsaw Pact in 1955. Germany itself was split in two, and the city of Berlin, deep inside East Germany, was divided as well. For the next four decades, two superpowers would compete everywhere on Earth, but never fight each other directly. Why was it “cold”? The answer is on the next slide.
